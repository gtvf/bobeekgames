Privacy Policy

Podstakannik Rails — last updated 2 October 2026

Podstakannik Rails is developed and published by Bobeek Games ("we", "us"). This policy explains what happens to your data when you play the game. It is short because the game collects very little.

We run no game servers

Multiplayer sessions are hosted by one of the players and connect through Steam. We do not operate game servers and we do not store anything about you on our side. The only data that reaches us is the diagnostics described below — a technical record of each session, plus crash and error reports — which Unity stores on our behalf.

What is processed while you play

  • Your Steam ID and Steam display name. Used to place you in a lobby and to show who is who. Other players in your session can see your display name above your character. This is handled by Steam; it is required for multiplayer to work at all.
  • Your voice, while your microphone is open — while you hold the talk key, after you toggle it on, or all the time in voice-activation mode, as you choose in the settings. Voice chat is provided by Vivox, part of Unity Gaming Services. Your audio is transmitted to the other players in your session in real time. We do not record, store, or listen to it, and neither the game nor we keep any copy. Your Steam ID and display name are passed to Vivox so voices can be matched to players.
  • Your settings and character choices. Volume levels, graphics options, key bindings, chosen language, and your character's appearance are saved on your own computer through Unity's PlayerPrefs. They never leave your device and are never sent to us.
  • The compartment TV. On every train ride — whether or not anyone puts a video on — the Steam client's built-in browser loads the TV's small web page from GitHub Pages (gtvf.github.io), and that page loads YouTube's embedded player. GitHub and YouTube (Google) therefore see your IP address and ordinary browser information on every ride, and the player may store what YouTube's own embedded player stores, each under its own privacy policy.
  • YouTube links you enter on the compartment TV. The ID of a video you type in is sent to the other players in your session so their TV shows the same video. The video itself is loaded by the Steam client's built-in browser directly from YouTube, as if you had opened the page yourself. We receive nothing; the game keeps only the current video ID for the ride.
  • Diagnostics (session records, crash and error reports). The game uses Unity Diagnostics, a Unity service. Each time you play, it sends Unity a technical record of the session: your device model, operating system, CPU and graphics card, the game and Unity versions, how long the session lasted and how much memory it used, together with an ID unique to your installation of the game; like any online service, Unity also sees your IP address. If the game crashes or hits an error, it also sends a report of where in the game's code this happened and the last few lines of the game's log. Those lines can include your Steam display name, the Steam IDs of you and other players, lobby IDs, file paths on your computer (which can contain your Windows user name) and the names of music files in the game's music folder. We read these reports in Unity's dashboard only to find and fix bugs, relying on our legitimate interest in keeping the game stable. Unity keeps them for 90 days. There is no switch for this inside the game.

What we do not do

  • No gameplay analytics: we do not track what you do in the game. The diagnostics above hold only technical data and, after an error, the last few lines of the game's log.
  • No advertising and no advertising identifiers.
  • No accounts to create and no passwords — the game uses your existing Steam session.
  • No payment information reaches us; all purchases are handled by Steam.
  • No selling or sharing of personal data with anyone.

Third parties

Four services are involved in running the game, each under its own privacy policy:

  • Valve Corporation (Steam) — distribution, ownership, friends, lobbies and matchmaking. See the Steam Privacy Policy.
  • Unity Technologies (Vivox, Unity Diagnostics) — voice chat and diagnostics. Unity processes the voice stream and the diagnostics and may collect technical identifiers needed to operate these services. See the Unity Game Player and App User Privacy Policy.
  • GitHub, Inc. (GitHub Pages) — hosts the web page the compartment TV displays; it is downloaded on every train ride. See the GitHub General Privacy Statement.
  • Google (YouTube) — the video shown on the compartment TV. The embedded player loads on every train ride, runs in the Steam client's browser and talks to YouTube directly. See the Google Privacy Policy and the YouTube Terms of Service.

Children

The game is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect data from them. Because voice chat is not moderated or filtered, parents should be aware that players can hear each other speak freely.

Your rights

We hold no data about you on our own side. Diagnostics are stored for us by Unity and deleted after 90 days. If you want them deleted sooner, write to us: the game does not show which reports come from your computer, so we will delete the stored reports of all players. Requests concerning your Steam account should go to Valve, requests concerning voice service data to Unity, and requests concerning what the compartment TV loads to GitHub or Google, using the policies linked above. If you are unsure where to ask, write to us and we will point you in the right direction.

Changes

If the game starts processing anything else, this page will be updated before that version ships, and the date at the top will change.
